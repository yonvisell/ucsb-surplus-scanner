# UCSB disposal and recycling guidance

This app captures equipment details and records review status. It does not determine whether an item is inventorial, approve its disposal, certify storage-media sanitization, or schedule collection.

## Inventory and surplus routing

Use the UCSB asset number or property tag as an inventory clue and confirm status through the department's equipment custodian or UCSB Equipment Management. The asset-tag field is not a legal classification. EIMR is for records with an assigned UCSB Asset number; the current EIMR disposal path is **Transfer** with **Surplus Sales** selected as the modification. The EIMR must be submitted before a Furniture Services Work Request. UCSB Surplus Sales directs departments to consult it about property without a UCSB asset number and does not authorize departments to sell surplus property directly. Do not infer disposal eligibility from purchase price alone.

Sources: [EIMR forms and instructions](https://bfs.ucsb.edu/equipment/forms), [UCSB departments: Surplus Sales](https://www.dls.ucsb.edu/surplus-sales/ucsb-departments), [equipment disposal](https://bfs.ucsb.edu/equipment/disposal), and [inventory updates](https://bfs.ucsb.edu/equipment/inventory-updates).

## E-waste, size, and hazardous exceptions

UCSB's DLS e-waste guidance describes scheduled Furniture Services pickup/drop-off for inventorial and larger devices, and AS Recycling campus bins for smaller everyday e-waste. Items over **2 ft × 2 ft × 2 ft** or **50 lb** should be scheduled for Furniture Services pickup. Do not put e-waste in ordinary trash. Specialized or hazardous electronic/chemical waste (examples include mercury devices, lab-grade freezers, biological waste, chemicals, sharps, and radioactive material) must go through EH&S; some equipment may require additional EH&S approvals. A department should confirm the proper destination when an item is contaminated, hazardous, unusually large, or otherwise outside the small-bin description.

Sources: [UCSB E-waste Recycling & Disposal](https://www.dls.ucsb.edu/furniture-services/ucsb-e-waste-recycling-disposal) and [Electronic Waste at UCSB](https://www.dfss.ucsb.edu/facilities-management/refuse-recycling/electronic-waste-ucsb).

## Storage media

DLS says a campus IT specialist may leave a computer drive installed for e-waste collection only when they can verify it was **completely wiped** and mark the item accordingly. Otherwise, the drive will be destroyed. The cited UCSB collection guidance does not prescribe a wiping standard or method. The app records a verifier, date, and method/reference when staff mark a wipe as verified; that record is a departmental attestation, not an app certification or independent proof. Ask departmental IT to determine and perform the appropriate sanitization.

Source: [UCSB E-waste Recycling & Disposal](https://www.dls.ucsb.edu/furniture-services/ucsb-e-waste-recycling-disposal).

## Batteries

UCSB's public e-waste guidance lists batteries as accepted in the campus e-waste stream. Separately, EH&S's used-battery factsheet says batteries generated by UCSB departments are universal waste to be sent to EH&S, stored in a sturdy labeled container with the date first generated, and transferred within nine months. Because those pages address different collection contexts and are not fully harmonized, this app flags battery handling for staff confirmation; loose department-generated batteries should be routed through the EH&S waste process. Do not put batteries in ordinary trash. EH&S identifies changes in shape or color, leaking, odor, odd sounds, unusual heat, and smoking as warning signs for failing lithium-ion batteries; seek EH&S direction for a suspect battery rather than treating it as ordinary loose e-waste.

Sources: [EH&S used batteries factsheet](https://www.ehs.ucsb.edu/sites/default/files/docs/hw/usedbattery.pdf), [UCSB E-waste Recycling & Disposal](https://www.dls.ucsb.edu/furniture-services/ucsb-e-waste-recycling-disposal), [DFSS electronic waste](https://www.dfss.ucsb.edu/facilities-management/refuse-recycling/electronic-waste-ucsb), and [EH&S lithium-ion battery safety checklist](https://www.ehs.ucsb.edu/sites/default/files/docs/ls/Lithium%20Battery%20Safety%20Checklist.pdf).

## Export and identifier handling

JSON backup preserves the stored record structure and raw capture strings for restoration. Import merges without overwriting: identical records are counted as skipped, while changed IDs, identifier collisions, or conflicting parent batches are counted as conflicts and left untouched. A backup asset is restored only when its parent batch exists locally or in that backup.

CSV has one header row and repeats export time and batch details on each item row, including item creation and update times and the original capture values as JSON. CSV is a review/reporting format; spreadsheet software can change identifier cells during import, including removing leading zeroes. To preserve serials and asset tags, import CSV columns as text or use the JSON backup for round-tripping. The app trims surrounding whitespace from the saved serial/tag fields but retains each original capture value separately.
